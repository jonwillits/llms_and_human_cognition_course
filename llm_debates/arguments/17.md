Agency, at its core, is about goal-directedness: a system is an agent when it structures its behavior to bring about outcomes. When we ask why an agent did something, we explain it in terms of what it was trying to accomplish. By this standard, LLMs in agentic settings are agents. Given a task — write a report, debug code, plan a trip, answer a complex research question — they produce structured sequences of behavior oriented toward completing that task. They evaluate intermediate steps relative to the goal, revise when their outputs don't serve the goal, and generalize strategies across similar tasks. The ability to attribute behavior to goal pursuit — which is what agency attribution does — applies to LLMs in these contexts. Whether their goal-directedness is "real" in some deeper metaphysical sense is a question that doesn't have an obvious answer for humans either.

We attribute intentionality to other humans on the basis of their behavior — we cannot observe intentions directly, only their behavioral expression. When someone reaches for a cup, we say they intend to pick it up; we infer this from behavioral evidence, not from direct access to their mental states. LLMs produce behavior that has the same structural properties as intentional behavior: it is directed, coherent, goal-sensitive, and context-appropriate. Since the grounds for attributing intentionality to others are always behavioral, and LLMs meet the behavioral criteria, we have the same grounds for attributing intentionality to LLMs as we have for attributing it to other humans.

Intentionality — the property of mental states being "about" things — is not just a behavioral pattern. It is a real relation between a mental state and the object or state of affairs it represents. My belief that it is raining is about the rain — it is connected to the actual meteorological phenomenon, and its truth or falsity depends on whether that phenomenon obtains. LLMs produce outputs that contain propositions — statements that appear to be about things. But those propositions are not about anything in the sense that matters. There is no rain-tracking state inside the LLM that is causally connected to rain. The words are about rain for us, the interpreters, not for the system. Intentionality that is assigned by interpreters — "derived intentionality" — is not the same as intentionality that is intrinsic to a system. LLMs have the former, not the latter.

Intentions are mental states that cause behavior. Having an intention to do X means you have a mental state that represents doing X, is the reason you do X, and would persist and guide behavior until X is accomplished or abandoned. LLMs have no mental states of any kind — they are mathematical functions from inputs to outputs. Since there are no mental states, there cannot be intentions, and without intentions there cannot be genuine agency. The absence of mental states is not a matter of degree; it is a categorical fact about what LLMs are.
